Survival of the Gummiest

A natural selection simulation. Hunt a population of gummy bears for seven rounds of predation and watch which traits survive.

This is a digital version of the gummy bear natural selection lab from class. Each part changes what the trait under selection is: colour and habitat, speed and agility, size, and finally all three at once. Every trial you run — from any part — is added to the lab log at the bottom of the page for your report.

The four parts are independent — do them in any order: Part 1 Part 2 Part 3 Part 4

Part 1 — Camouflage

Colour is the trait under selection. A population of 50 bears is released into a habitat and drifts slowly across it — every bear moves at the same gentle pace, so the only thing that changes your chances of catching one is how well it blends in.

Part 2 — Speed and agility

Colour no longer matters — every bear looks identical. Half are fast and half are slow, and you won't know which is which until the round starts and they begin to move at their own constant speed. Being hard to catch, not hard to see, is the trait under selection.

1. Write your hypothesis

Predict what will happen to fast and slow bears after seven rounds of predation, e.g. "Fast bears will become the majority because they are harder to catch before they move away."

Part 3 — Size

Now size is the trait under selection. Every bear is the same colour and drifts at the same slow, constant speed as in Part 1 — the only difference between them is how big a target they are. Large bears are easy to grab; small bears are much harder to pin down.

1. Write your hypothesis

Predict what will happen to large and small bears after seven rounds of predation, e.g. "Small bears will become the majority because they are harder to see and catch."

Part 4 — All pressures combined

Real populations rarely face just one kind of pressure at a time. Here, colour, speed, and size all vary at once and are assigned independently — a bear's colour tells you nothing about how fast or big it is. Choose a habitat as in Part 1; every bear also has its own speed and size. Results are reported separately for each trait, so you can see whether camouflage, speed, or size ends up mattering most.

Based on the class gummy bear natural selection lab. Every population starts at 50 bears and runs for seven rounds of predation across eight generations; survivors reproduce at a rate of 1.1 per round, so a trait that hits zero is extinct for good. Each trial ends with a short quiz on your own data before you can start another.
